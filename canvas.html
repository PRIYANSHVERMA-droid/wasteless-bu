1 SDG CHALLENGE
Which SDG are you addressing?
Primary: SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption & Production) — Target 12.3: Halve per capita food waste.
Secondary Alignment: SDG 11 (Sustainable Communities) & SDG 13 (Climate Action).
What real-world problem are students solving?
Avoidable plate waste in Bennett dining halls: 41.7% leave food regularly, driven by taste mismatches (28.3%) & 1 PM rush (28.3%), lacking portion cues before loading full trays.
2 STUDENT EXPERIENCE
What will students DO in this project?
(Activities, methods, key experiences)
1. Administer verified 60-student survey across hostel blocks.
2. Audit 3 baseline weekday lunch services deducting tare weights.
3. Interview dining managers & kitchen staff on operational barriers.
4. Deploy table nudges, 1-spoon taste checks, and digital QR pledge.
5. Quantify post-pilot plate-waste reduction with transparent reflection.
3 STUDENT DELIVERABLE
What will students CREATE or DELIVER by the end of the project?
• WasteLess BU Web Hub: Real-time WebSocket pledge sync & interactive impact calculator.
• 4 Print Campaign Posters: Portion cues & 1-spoon taste-check cards.
• 20-Sec Digital Pledge System: Live cross-campus commitment counter.
• Academic Dossier & Pitch Deck: Triangulated data documentation.

6 IMPACT
How will you know the project succeeded?
(One sentence)
Verified when normalized plate waste (g/meal) declines measurably across comparable lunch shifts while digital pledge engagement spreads campus-wide.
What measurable impact will your project create?
(One key metric)
Key Metric: 22.4% reduction in plate waste (102.5 → 79.5 g/meal), saving ~23 kg/lunch shift with 65+ signed peer commitments.
7 TWIST ADAPTATION
After receiving the Impact Twist Card, how did your project change?
Kitchen ladle rush during 1:00 PM peak created bottle-necks; eliminated intrusive weighing queues to prevent crowding.
What key changes did you make?
Pivoted to passive dish-drop observation tallies + table-top "Start Half, Refill Free" prompt cards & digital QR pledges, ensuring seamless dining flow.
8 YOU AS A MENTOR
In one sentence, define the role you as a mentor will play.
A Socratic catalyst and methodology auditor—challenging assumptions, instilling scout mindset, and demanding empirical evidence while students lead all fieldwork.
How will you empower students rather than provide answers?
By asking probing questions ("Why do 56.7% cite rush & taste?"), guiding course corrections during operational friction, and fostering reflection over quick answers.
