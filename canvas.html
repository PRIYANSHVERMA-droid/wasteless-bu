1 SDG CHALLENGE
Which SDG are you addressing?
Primary: SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption & Production) — Target 12.3: Halve food waste by 2030.
Secondary Alignment: SDG 11 (Sustainable Communities) & SDG 13 (Climate Action).
What real-world problem are students solving?
Avoidable plate waste in Bennett dining halls: 41.7% of surveyed students leave leftovers regularly, driven by taste mismatches (28.3%) and peak 1 PM class rush (28.3%), lacking portion feedback before loading full plates.
2 STUDENT EXPERIENCE
What will students DO in this project?
(Activities, methods, key experiences)
1. Administer verified 60-student survey across hostel blocks & schools.
2. Audit 3 baseline weekday lunch services deducting tare weights (g/meal).
3. Interview dining managers & serving staff on operational barriers.
4. Design & test table-top prompts, 1-spoon taste checks, and portion cues.
5. Deploy QR-linked digital pledge and monitor cross-campus real-time sync.
6. Quantify post-pilot plate-waste reduction and reflect honestly on limitations.
3 STUDENT DELIVERABLE
What will students CREATE or DELIVER by the end of the project?
• WasteLess BU Web Hub: Responsive HTML/CSS/JS site with real-time WebSocket pledge sync & interactive data calculator.
• 4 Print-Ready Campaign Posters: Portion nudges, 1-spoon taste-check table cards, and high-contrast QR display boards.
• 20-Second Digital Pledge System: Anonymous signer flow with custom canvas initials & downloadable certificate PNGs.
• Comprehensive Documentation: 8-page academic dossier & 12-slide pitch deck with verified data triangulations.

6 IMPACT
How will you know the project succeeded?
(One sentence)
Success is verified when normalized edible plate waste (g/meal) declines measurably across comparable lunch services while student pledge engagement reaches active campus adoption.
What measurable impact will your project create?
(One key metric)
Key Metric: 22.4% reduction in normalized plate waste (from 102.5 g/meal down to 79.5 g/meal), saving ~23 kg per lunch slot with 65+ signed student commitments.
7 TWIST ADAPTATION
After receiving the Impact Twist Card, how did your project change?
When dining staff flagged kitchen ladle bottlenecks during the 1:00 PM rush, we eliminated intrusive weighing queues to prevent crowding.
What key changes did you make?
Pivoted to passive dish-drop observation tallies paired with self-service "Start Half, Refill Free" prompt cards and QR pledges at tables, preserving high data integrity without delaying lines.
8 YOU AS A MENTOR
In one sentence, define the role you as a mentor will play.
A Socratic catalyst and methodology auditor—challenging assumptions, instilling scout mindset, and demanding empirical evidence while students lead all field implementation.
How will you empower students rather than provide answers?
By asking probing questions ("What if 56.7% leave food due to rush, not appetite?"), facilitating course corrections when plans hit dining friction, and guiding reflection over answers.
