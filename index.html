Evidence Before Action: Scope & Research Protocol

Rather than assuming why students leave meals unfinished, WasteLess BU isolates edible plate waste in one dining space and triangulates survey, observation, weighing, and staff evidence before deploying nudges.

Strict Operational Boundaries

Location & Audience One selected Bennett University venue (Main Student Dining Hall / Mess); students dining during comparable weekday lunch periods.
Included Waste Eligible edible plate waste only — cooked food left uneaten on student trays and plates.
Excluded Waste Kitchen preparation trimmings, serving-line surplus, bones/peels where separable, and non-food packaging are never combined into plate waste.
Measurement Rigour Digital scale readings net of container tare weight across 3 baseline and 3 post-pilot sessions, or an authorised anonymous observation tally if scale access is restricted.

Three-Phase Evidence Triangulation

  • Phase A — Anonymous Student Survey (Target: 50–100 responses)

    8-question Google Form (or built-in anonymous survey) sampling across hostels, batches, and meal windows to diagnose frequency, top drivers, portion preferences, and display receptivity.

  • Phase B — Structured Dining Hall Observations & Weighing

    Timestamped field notes logging meal counts, peak rush surges (12:45–1:30 PM), commonly discarded items, and verified net plate-waste weights or plate tallies.

  • Phase C — Cafeteria Staff & Supervisor Interviews

    Five structured prompts exploring leftover hotspots, refill feasibility, and operational barriers. Staff views are logged strictly as qualitative context, never measured kilograms.

15-Day Execution Roadmap

  1. Days 1–2 Setup & Approvals

    Confirm 8-member roles, align dining slot scope, launch anonymous student survey.

  2. Days 3–5 Baseline Evidence

    Collect 50–100 survey responses, interview staff, complete 3 comparable baseline logs.

  3. Days 6–7 Analysis & Design

    Clean datasets, run BU100 bias checks, match behavioural nudges to top waste drivers.

  4. Days 8–10 Build & Approve

    Build WasteLess BU website & QR pledge, print 4 posters & table cards, finalise display plan.

  5. Days 11–13 Live Campus Pilot

    Run campaign across 3 comparable meals, log QR pledges, apply Twist if needed, measure post-pilot.

  6. Days 14–15 Evaluate & Present

    Compute normalised impact, document limitations, finalize Canvas, 8-page Dossier & 7.5-min pitch.

      Practical Portion Tips for Bennett Diners

      Small frictionless choices at the serving counter eliminate avoidable plate waste without leaving anyone hungry.

      1

      Start with Half, Refill Freely

      Survey findings consistently highlight oversized initial servings as the #1 driver of plate leftovers. Ask serving staff for a half-scoop of rice or one roti first — second helpings stay open.

      2

      Taste Unfamiliar Dishes Before Loading

      Menu mismatch is the second major cause of discarded food. Take a single spoonful of any new sabzi or curry first before committing to a full katori.

      3

      Match Your Plate to Your Lecture Clock

      Between 12:45 PM and 1:45 PM, class rush causes half-eaten plates to be abandoned. When you have under 15 minutes before class, take a lighter first serving you can comfortably finish.

        TREP Canvas, BU100 Readings & Methodological Integrity

        Structured project blueprint aligned to all five BU100 evaluation parameters (5 × 8 = 40 marks).

        Box 1 • Problem & SDG Alignment

        Avoidable Plate Waste (SDG 12)

        Students take more food than they consume in the Main Student Dining Hall / Mess without visibility into waste volume. Primary SDG 12; Secondary SDGs 11 & 13.

        Box 2 • Research & Evidence Plan

        Triangulated Field Inquiry

        Anonymous Google Form survey (n = 50–100), 3 baseline + 3 post-pilot comparable weekday lunch observations/weighings, and structured cafeteria staff interviews.

        Box 3 • Solution & Deliverables

        Behavioural Nudges + Web Hub

        Four print-ready campaign posters & table cards, “Start Half, Refill Free” prompts, responsive WasteLess BU website, QR-linked student pledge, and live impact dashboard.

        Box 4 • Core Competencies

        Six BMI TREP Capabilities

        Leadership, Critical Thinking, Communication, Research, Analytical Thinking, Creativity, Digital Literacy, and Adaptability mapped across all 8 roles.

        Box 5 • Engaged Campus Resources

        Verified Bennett Stakeholders

        Only genuinely consulted resources: Dining Hall / Mess supervisors & serving staff, BU100 Faculty Mentor, and Campus Sustainability / Student Affairs approvals.

        Box 6 • Impact Metrics

        Normalised Reduction + Reach

        Primary: % change in normalised Waste per Meal (g). Supporting: valid survey n, QR scans & site visits, pre/post awareness shift, and digital pledge signatures.

        Box 7 • Twist Adaptation

        Protocol Pivot Readiness

        If direct bin weighing is restricted by dining operations, pivot seamlessly to an approved anonymous plate-leftover observation tally + survey evaluation without fabricating kg.

        Box 8 • Mentor Reflections

        Checkpoint Self-Correction

        Documented mid-sprint course corrections: separating kitchen prep from plate waste, controlling attendance via g/meal, and repositioning QR cards where students actually pause.

        1. Scout Mindset

        Investigated actual drivers of plate leftovers objectively rather than assuming student carelessness. When survey patterns point to portion sizing, unfamiliar menus, or lecture time crushes, the intervention shifts from guilt slogans to practical half-scoop and tasting nudges.

        2. Sampling & Confirmation Bias

        Avoided convenience sampling limited to close friends or eco-club volunteers. Survey distribution spans different meal times, hostels, and academic cohorts so findings represent typical Bennett dining habits rather than pre-confirmed assumptions.

        3. Evidence Checking & Factfulness

        Subtracted container tare weights, strictly separated kitchen preparation waste and packaging from edible plate waste, reported exact sample sizes (n), and used normalised per-meal averages (g/meal) instead of exaggerated total-weight claims.

        4. Uncertainty & Self-Correction

        Explicitly acknowledged menu changes, daily attendance shifts, and the short 3-day pilot window as confounding factors. Built mid-pilot feedback checks to relocate QR cards or pivot to observation tallies if constraints arise.

        Mandatory Ethical & Data Safeguards

        • Respectful Field Conduct: Follow dining operations norms before weighing bins or placing physical posters.
        • Zero Identifiable Student Photos: Photograph only trays, counters, displays, or consenting volunteers — never student faces.
        • Strict Survey Anonymity: Collect zero emails, roll numbers, or personally identifying fields in Google Forms.
        • Zero Data Fabrication: Report actual measured or tallied outcomes honestly — even a 0% shift earns high BU100 marks when rigorously analyzed.

        Honest Methodological Limitations

        • Single-Venue Scope: Findings apply strictly to Main Student Dining Hall / Mess — never extrapolated as a campus-wide reduction.
        • Menu Variability: Popular vs. less-preferred weekday menus naturally fluctuate plate leftovers across lunch slots.
        • Short Pilot Window: A 3-day post-pilot snapshot captures immediate nudge response rather than permanent semester-long habit formation.
        • Self-Report Bias: Survey responses and digital pledges measure stated intent alongside — not in place of — physical observations.
